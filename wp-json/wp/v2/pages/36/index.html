Binaural acousmatic film (8 minutes)<\/em><\/p>\n\n\n\n

This work aims to elicit a mode of listening similar to that originally described by Pierre Schaeffer as \u2018reduced listening\u2019, and later expanded upon by Francisco L\u00f3pez as \u2018profound listening\u2019, by employing an acousmatic strategy which separates the visual image from its accompanying sound source. The film itself is centred around what appears as a duality between the forest and the city, more specifically the differing modes of knowledge acquisition that occur in each environment. The definitiveness of this duality is brought into question via the promiscuity of the acousmatic space that emerges when moving images from one are intermingled with sound sources from the other. As these spaces bleed into each other, the possibility of listening to the sonic material detached from visual associations of source and meaning facilitates the discernment of irreducible traits inherent to the sonic matter itself; traits such as motion, impermanence and becoming. Henri Bergson described listening in this way as the knowing of a perceptive intuition that is incommunicable or non-transmissible linguistically. It is a type of knowledge that transcends the superficialities of logocentric abstraction and outer form, thereby disclosing an essential underlying flux inherent to all phenomena. This film is an invitation to engage our ears beyond the duration of the work itself. To intuitively discern these traits of sonic experiences in our daily lives; for the risk of not doing so is immobilisation, whilst the reward is an affirmation of life whereby, as Bergson describes: \u2018everything comes to life around us, everything is revivified in us\u2019.<\/p>\n","protected":false},"excerpt":{"rendered":"
